I'm Remington Braninburg—artist, father, coder, and survivor who creates from the margins.

My work doesn't come from comfort or privilege. It emerges from the raw intersection of survival and creativity, where most people never dare to look. I learned to code while living homeless in my car. I paint with the intensity of someone who's seen both the beauty and brutality of human experience. I build functional art that serves a purpose beyond decoration. I'm a polymath by necessity, not choice. When life strips everything away—home, stability, safety—you learn to build from whatever remains. Code becomes shelter. Art becomes oxygen. Each discipline feeds the others: street art informs canvas work, coding structures composition, writing provides the framework for everything. My bipolar disorder isn't a limitation—it's an amplifier. During manic phases, I absorb new skills at lightning speed and see connections others miss. In depressive phases, I create work that resonates with authentic human pain. My art swings between extremes because life does too. Everything I create is ultimately for my daughter. She needs to see persistence over perfection, to know that creativity survives any circumstances. I want her to understand that making something is always better than destroying something, that imagination is the one thing they can never take away.

My Philosophy

Experience without wisdom is worthless. What matters isn't how much you've been through, but how it shapes your vision and character. My experiences have given me a vision that extends beyond personal success to cultural impact. I want to prove that authenticity has commercial value, that depth has market appeal, that the most powerful art comes from places where polite society fears to look. I'm the chaos artist who brings order to destruction, creating work that matters regardless of circumstances. This isn't just about making art—it's about proving that creativity survives everything.
